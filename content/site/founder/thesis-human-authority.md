# Why NEO is built around human authority

## Header

**The question is not whether the system may act. It is who was entitled to let it.**

## Body

Most AI governance material answers one question: is this action permitted. That is necessary and it is half the problem.

The other half is entitlement. When an audit asks why an autonomous system did something consequential, "the policy allowed it" is not an answer, because the follow-up is who decided that policy applied here, were they authorised to decide it, and was that authorisation still valid at the moment the action was taken. A system that records permission without entitlement has produced a log that looks like accountability and is not.

That is why NEO separates the two, structurally, rather than treating authority as a field on a permission check. It costs more to build. It is the difference between a system an organisation can defend and one it can only explain.

I hold this as a design position rather than as a proven result, and the Program's papers tag it accordingly.
